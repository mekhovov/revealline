## Decision

Add **32 distinct lessons** to the existing 26: **eight Experienced, eight Advanced, eight Pro and eight Master**. Retain the recommended 14 Acro beginner lessons and 12 optional self-level lessons unchanged. The resulting school has **58 lessons**, of which **46 form the recommended Acro progression**; the full catalogue grows from 86 to **118 authored challenges**. Reversing a course, recolouring it, adding medals or increasing its difficulty does not count as another lesson.

The first **16 precision/navigation lessons use the existing objective types**. The following **16 aerobatic/combined lessons use the implemented versioned, replay-verified skill criteria**. Both groups now have complete actual command recordings; the publication and acceptance status is tracked below. A high gate does not prove a flip. An upright endpoint does not prove a roll. Four waypoints do not prove a complete orbit.

All lessons remain immediately selectable. Tiers recommend an order and explain prerequisites; they never lock content. Pro and Master are simulator curriculum names, not real-world qualifications. This is civilian and sport flight instruction; it introduces no combat or tactical material.

## Research decisions

- ORQA's developer-authored Flight School description progresses from fundamentals through Acro, throttle/cornering, obstacle manoeuvres and tricks. Adopt the gradual increase in combined skills, while retaining this project's already-approved Acro-first beginner track. [FPV SkyDive Flight School](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2511190/FPV_SkyDive__Flight_School/?l=english)
- VelociDrone documents visible transmitter inputs, slow ghost playback, next-gate direction indicators and comparison with a previous flight. Adopt readable example sticks, 0.25×/0.5× replay, clear gate direction and optional comparison with the player's own compatible recording. No online leaderboard or proprietary replay import is required. [VelociDrone manual](https://www.velocidrone.com/desktop_manual)
- Rotor Riot distinguishes basic rotation families, open-air exercises, object-based manoeuvres and contact tricks. Use familiar names for rolls, flips, yaw rotations, orbits, split-S, Immelmann and power loops. Keep contact tricks, Matty flips and backwards dives outside this increment until their collision and trajectory assumptions are qualified. The proposed course geometry and success thresholds below are original designs for this simulator, not copied training recipes. [Rotor Riot FPV Freestyle Tricktionary](https://rotorriot.com/blogs/tutorials-guides/fpv-freestyle-tricktionary)

These sources support a teaching structure and vocabulary. They do not establish physical equivalence between this integrator and a particular real quad, nor validate the proposed thresholds. Source-specific video, map, model and soundtrack assets are not being imported or redistributed.

## A continuous learning experience

The player stays in one coherent lesson view: **watch → move a control to take over → practise the current objective → receive a short success cue → continue to the next objective**. No compulsory menu, explanation modal or Start button should interrupt every successful step.

1. Begin with a short, faithful example at a labelled teaching pace. The drone's actual orientation, position, momentum and stick commands remain linked. Display example input distinctly from player input; never enlarge small live commands to fill a gimbal.
2. Deliberate keyboard/touch input or calibrated radio movement takes control. Neutral/baseline filtering prevents a resting throttle, noisy axis or a held input from a previous screen stealing the example. Focus, disconnect, graphics loss and Escape retain the established safe pause rules.
3. Keep the immediate task, target cue and optional drone/receiver diagram visible. Detailed explanation is available on demand. Voice is optional; every cue has EN/UK text and a non-colour-only visual equivalent.
4. An example may loop; manual practice remains under the player's control and does not automatically reset or return to an example. Replay example is explicit. Reduced motion suppresses involuntary looping but still permits deliberate playback.
5. Successful **player-owned** task completion advances the coach to the next objective automatically. Keep the flight state continuous when the next task shares the same flight. Explain a required landing or repositioning as a real next task; do not teleport between achievements.
6. At lesson completion, show a quiet completion panel with Retry, Practise weakest step and Continue. A persistent optional auto-continue setting may start the next lesson's explanation once the current drone is safely stopped; it must not automatically arm a scored flight or resume held controls. Pause/Exit remain available in fullscreen.

There are two truthful progress types. A checkpoint practice session can remember learned steps, with provenance marked as practice. A complete scored lesson requires a fresh, contiguous, player-owned attempt and successful replay. Watching or taking over halfway through an example must not manufacture a full-lesson medal. Where a checkpoint starts from a demonstration, reconstruct it from exact dependencies and the recorded command prefix; record the boundary explicitly. Never treat example-owned input as player input. Existing v1/v2 proofs remain executable.

## Authoring conventions for the following tables

All first-wave tasks use Acro/FPV/Gentle as the recommended setting, ground starts and real landings. Each includes 4–10 learning steps, a complete demonstration and checkpoint examples. A typical learning session is 4–8 minutes; later composite lessons may be 6–12 minutes. The scored attempt still respects the existing twelve-minute ceiling.

Initial dimensions are deliberately generous. A “settle” target below means a 2 m-radius box unless otherwise specified, ≤1.5 m/s total speed, ≤20° hold tilt and a 1-second dwell. A “land” means the named physical support surface, ≤1.2 m/s touchdown speed, ≤15° touchdown tilt, throttle ≤10%, then 0.6 seconds supported. Both are stricter than some current beginner lessons but use already-supported criteria. Heading targets use the current ±15° tolerance. Gates generally begin 4 m wide × 3 m high; visual frames and collision must agree. Measurements in the tables are criteria targets, not assertions of completed verification.

Learning feedback may describe an ideal path without pretending it is an enforced condition. In wave one, for example, a green gate followed by a settled target proves the crossing and the stop; it does not prove a minimum entry speed or continuous speed band between them. No-contact medals, minimum-speed conditions, continuous corridors and path-quality scoring wait for the new skill evaluator.

## Skill evaluator prerequisite for wave two

Implemented as `rotation-v1`, `attitude-v1`, `path-v1` and `crossing-v1`; the [final schema](fpv-skill-objectives.md) defines the exact fields and bounds. Course identity includes these criteria, while all previous v1/v2 behavior remains compatible. The following design acceptance semantics explain that implementation.

- **Ordered attitude events:** use the continuous quaternion orientation and body vectors. Identify signed rotation about the intended reference/body axis, ordered quarter-turn checkpoints, full winding, acceptable cross-axis error and final stabilized attitude. Handle quaternion sign equivalence and yaw wrap. Endpoint subtraction of Euler angles is insufficient.
- **Spatial trajectory events:** ordered volume/crossing conditions, optional y-plane crossings, signed winding around an authored point/axis, height difference, travel-direction change and bounded corridor/radius where required. Track crossings per fixed tick using previous and current state; prevent skip-through and boundary jitter from farming events.
- **Composite segments:** bind an attitude sequence to a spatial segment, minimum recovery height and optional contact-free period; reset the segment accumulator when its stated validity conditions fail. A failed segment offers a retry and useful feedback, not an unexplained permanent lock.
- **Replay authority:** sample states from actual canonical-command simulation, never from rendered pixels, UI timestamps, stick illustrations or camera orientation. Example segments, checkpoint reconstruction and player-owned scoring are explicitly separate. Practice progress cannot become a verified medal without the appropriate complete attempt.
- **Stable tolerances:** use fixed units, deterministic arithmetic or an explicitly qualified deterministic implementation. Thresholds, rate profile, seed, visibility assistance and evaluator revision are part of content compatibility. Do not silently change the existing Gentle response or all world physics to make a trick easier.
- **False-positive qualification:** plain upright flight, flying the route without rotation, rotating on the wrong axis, repeated ±small-angle wiggles, inverted endpoints without the required path, the wrong turn direction, a bounce off a surface and an example-only run must not satisfy the corresponding trick.

Before authoring wave two, qualify one roll, one pitch loop, one yaw winding and one position orbit in both directions with actual command replays. Check full inversion, heading ±180° wrapping and near-vertical attitudes. If an exercise is impractical at the current rates, revise its geometry or choose an explicit existing supported response profile; defer it if the model cannot represent the taught motion credibly.

## Bilingual content and demonstration contract

Each new lesson requires an EN and UK title, summary, prerequisite explanation, per-step action, reason, success cue, common-error cue, recovery hint and control labels for the chosen Mode 1–4. Use axis names plus physical meaning: pitch / тангаж (нахил носа), roll / крен, yaw / курс (поворот носа), throttle / газ (тяга). Avoid assuming that pitch is on the right stick for every radio layout. Ukrainian technique names may retain a familiar English term beside the translation where useful. A fluent human reviewer must check final instructional Ukrainian before declaring language acceptance.

Example first-wave step:

- EN action: “Pass the blue gate, then tilt back gently to slow down. Settle inside the outlined box.”
- UK action: “Пройди сині ворота, потім плавно нахили дрон назад, щоб сповільнитися. Стабілізуй політ у позначеній зоні.”
- EN reason: “Levelling stops the tilt, not your existing motion. Counter-tilt changes that motion.”
- UK reason: “Вирівнювання прибирає нахил, але не зупиняє рух. Зворотний нахил допомагає його сповільнити.”
- EN success: “Steady inside the box — next, turn toward the shelter.”
- UK success: “Дрон стабільний у зоні — тепер поверни ніс до навісу.”

Example aerobatic step:

- EN action: “Enter the open box at the marked height. Roll all the way around, then stop the rotation and recover.”
- UK action: “Увійди у вільну зону на позначеній висоті. Виконай повний оберт за креном, зупини обертання й віднови рівний політ.”
- EN reason: “The course checks the entire rotation and the recovery, not only an upright finish.”
- UK reason: “Урок перевіряє весь оберт і відновлення польоту, а не лише рівне положення наприкінці.”

A **minimum of 32 complete, successful Acro demonstration recordings** is required for the 32 new lessons. Every recording must replay to the same verified completion and be portable with its exact course, evaluator, model and response identities. A recording at normal physics can be viewed at 1×, 0.5× or 0.25×; playback speed changes presentation time, not commands or simulated physics. Use marked segments of those recordings as teaching examples where possible. Each meaningful step has an example segment or an actual isolated integrator exercise, not a decorative animation presented as a flown demonstration.

Ghost playback and example sticks are optional aids. Keep a player-selected assistance setting stable during a scored attempt and include it in compatibility where it affects navigation. The coach must never label a ghost's inputs as the player's. Recordings demonstrated with a controlled synthetic controller qualify the code path; they do not establish physical-radio acceptance.

## Functional acceptance required before a lesson is complete

1. The route is distinct, has physical clearance and can be completed from its real spawn using the pinned input response; no hidden teleport, forced example input or collision bypass.
2. Its full command recording completes and replays under exact content dependencies. Original 24 Academy demonstrations and all existing school identities/proofs remain compatible.
3. The continuous guide uses player-owned state for objective handoff. Watching, looping, pausing, takeover after an example and checkpoint resets do not award full-course completion.

## Explicit limits

This expansion does not teach or certify airspace law, real hardware assembly, radio-frequency management, battery safety, field-site assessment, emergency procedures, real aerodynamics or professional filming operations. Such topics require separate qualified instruction. Avoid implying realistic wind, propwash, battery sag, damaged props, GPS rescue or a real drone's aerodynamic stall if the runtime does not simulate them. No contact tricks, backwards dives, arbitrary imported scripts, new vehicle physics or copied commercial simulator maps are needed for these 32 lessons.
