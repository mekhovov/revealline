# Analog reception and menu motion references

Checked 2026-09-29. This note refines the earlier [flight references](analog-flight-references-2026-09-29.md): retain the recognizable, already-concealed game image and degrade its reception, matching the existing jammer treatment. A uniform grey overlay loses that connection. Recommendations below are art direction, not a calibrated RF model or hardware timing specification.

## Primary evidence

- [TBS Fusion receiver](https://www.team-blacksheep.com/products/prod:tbs_fusion): The manufacturer identifies colour deterioration and video desynchronization at the reception limit. This supports weakening colour and picture registration separately. It does not prescribe a universal sequence or duration.

- [SKYZONE SteadyView X V2 manual](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0597/9668/4956/files/V2_5.8G_260826.pdf?v=1789974888), linked by its [official download page](https://www.skyzonefpv.com/pages/download): The English quick-start text on PDF page 9 describes darkening, colour distortion and a rolling image when nonstandard camera signals confuse Mix mode. The Chinese reception-mode explanation on PDF page 4 discusses synchronization loss and edge displacement. These are receiver-specific behaviours, not evidence that every weak signal rolls.

- [Analog Devices AN-1603](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/app-notes/an-1603.html): Composite video contains luma, chroma and synchronization. Horizontal blanking contains the sync pulse and colour burst; chroma phase relative to burst determines hue. Separating desaturation, limited chroma instability and line displacement is technically motivated. This source describes signal structure, not an observed FPV failure animation.

- [AnalogDepth, v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24312), [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24312): Verified directly; submitted 21 September 2026. Section III-B describes real analog FPV recordings with spatially structured noise and sharp band artifacts that Gaussian synthesis does not reproduce. Figure 1's caption identifies horizontal noise bands; Figure 3 contrasts synthesized noise and a recorded residual. Suitable spatial inspiration for clustered impulse streaks. It is a recent preprint about depth estimation, not evidence for reception-transition timing, a universal monochrome palette, or validation of our procedural implementation. No paper images or recorded noise are incorporated into the game.

- [John Seaber's firsthand rapidFIRE investigation](https://blog.jseaber.com/2018/10/05/solving-unreliable-fat-shark-l1-mod-w-rapidfire/): Describes rolling/tearing before lock under a specific overheating/power problem and links [his tearing/no-lock recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LO800H-hhJ4). Useful temporal reference to review visually; its long lock delay should not be copied into menu navigation. Article and video link checked; the video was not independently watched in this research pass.

The AnalogDepth text and figure captions were checked. The PDF screenshot endpoint did not expose image pixels to this research session, so this note does not claim independent visual inspection of its figures.

## Official interaction examples

- [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117) calls for ways to disable, pause or hide nonessential moving UI content. Its **HyperDot** example makes menu background objects static when background animation is disabled; its **Microsoft Store** example separates video autoplay and sound preferences. It also explains how opaque text backgrounds can improve readability over unavoidable movement. These are Microsoft's documented examples, not games independently tested here.
- [Material's official Android motion documentation](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Motion.md#transitions) uses **fade** for menus/dialogs and **fade through** for destinations without a strong relationship. Its linked examples include normal/slow GIF galleries. The documented fade defaults are 150 ms in/75 ms out; fade-through defaults to 300 ms. These are component defaults, not a requirement for game menus.
- [Google's Reply motion codelab](https://developer.android.com/codelabs/material-motion-android) provides a concrete example: mailbox changes use fade-through; opening an email uses a container transform. The explanation and code were checked; the embedded demonstration was not independently played.
- [Material's older duration guidance](https://m1.material.io/motion/duration-easing.html) recommends short, responsive transitions and gives 150–200 ms for desktop. This is explicitly legacy guidance. Current M3 motion pages required JavaScript and yielded no readable body through the research tool, so their latest timing guidance is not inferred from this older page.

## Recommendations for RevealLine

1. **Keep the actual received scene.** Use the already-masked board as the input, weaken its colour, add the existing monochrome clustered noise and a few short horizontal disturbances. Keep some scene structure perceptible during loss. At acquisition, draw terrain and actors clearly above the effect; at terminal failure, the complete frozen arena may degrade. Never sample the unrevealed original to reconstruct missing pixels.
2. **Distinguish the cues.** Snow provides fine interference; clustered streaks provide spatial structure; a short displaced band suggests horizontal sync trouble. A single small vertical slip can be an optional stronger loss cue, but continuous full-screen rolling is distracting. Treat colour-burst trouble as restrained colour instability, not giant RGB-separated silhouettes. Avoid default tape scratches, VHS head-switching bars, curved CRT glass or a collapsing bright dot: those suggest other equipment.
3. **Make menu retuning one brief accent.** A proposed 220 ms pulse on deliberate major-menu changes fits the short-transition references without claiming a prescribed standard. Restrict the interference to decorative background pixels. Keep labels, focus indicators and controls stable and usable immediately; replace an in-flight pulse when a newer navigation occurs. Do not replay it for every focus move, slider change, hover or ordinary Pause/Resume. Let the resting menu settle completely.
4. **Give reduced motion a genuinely still result.** Skip positional slips and rolling; use an immediate change or static desaturated/noisy image. The menu effect should disappear entirely when motion is disabled. Keep background animation and audio preferences independent, and retain opaque, legible controls. Avoid bright full-frame alternation and cycling between a clear image and interference.

The proposed 220 ms accent and the choice of sparse, low-contrast disturbances are implementation recommendations. The references establish useful visual and interaction principles; they do not substitute for browser review, player feedback, or physical-display accessibility testing.
