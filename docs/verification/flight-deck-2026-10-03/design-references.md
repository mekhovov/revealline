# Flight Deck material direction — 2026-10-03

Research and original art direction for the default Flight Deck theme (formerly Industrial Workshop), Ember Foundry, and Polar Relay. This document distinguishes source observations from proposed implementation. It is not a visual acceptance or performance report. Reference artwork is viewed for research only; no game textures, icons, symbols, or models are copied into the project.

## Three distinct industrial themes

| Theme             | Material hierarchy                                                                                                                                        | Accent and motion                                                                                                                                     | Description direction                                                                     |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Flight Deck**   | Graphite powder-coated backing; machined gray steel caps; black instrument wells; warm ivory legends.                                                     | Amber signals and short mechanical press travel. Small fasteners, grip ribs, and vent recesses supply identity.                                       | The game's main flight console: graphite steel, warm amber signals, and tactile controls. |
| **Ember Foundry** | Warm charcoal cast iron; burnished copper rims; soot-dark inset wells. Grain should be slightly coarser than Flight Deck, confined by engineered borders. | Copper-orange activation; narrow furnace seams or heat slots only at structural edges. Use the same restrained mechanical press behavior.             | A night-shift foundry in cast iron, copper, and furnace amber.                            |
| **Polar Relay**   | Blue-black enameled instruments; pale alloy lips; deep rubber seals. Finer finish and cleaner manufactured edges distinguish it from Ember.               | Ice-cyan signals; precise cool highlights; sparse frost in external corners. No falling snow, blinking background, or animated scanlines on controls. | A remote polar station with midnight enamel, icy alloy, and cyan telemetry.               |

Names, palettes, motifs, and construction here are original design choices. They interpret the sources; they are not official styles from those games.

## Runtime review priorities

1. Inspect idle, hover, pressed, selected, destructive, disabled, loading, and focus together at actual 44-pixel player and 32-pixel fine-pointer Studio control sizes. Repeat with large text and narrow layouts.
2. Give the button a fixed physical construction: stable grain, a thin upper highlight, side lip, lower contact shadow, and a recessed surrounding well. Press travel must not shift layout or pointer targets.
3. Keep foreground/background state pairs atomic. A selected disabled control must remain disabled; a pressed destructive control must retain its warning pair.
4. Make ornaments Off, high contrast, forced colors, and reduced motion remove the corresponding detail or movement. Verify nested Studio previews do not inherit another theme's finish.
5. Compare ordinary screens and authoring previews, not only theme cards. Check menus, settings, pause/results, dialogs, sliders, mission cards, SIM overlays, and the Studio specimen.
6. Reuse the existing immutable revision and semantic binding contracts. Renaming a display label must not invalidate recorded appearances or content identities. New default policy must respect deliberate saved selections and campaign/community choices.

The existing material generator already provides deterministic fixed-size powder, brush, thread, timber, stone, and fiber tiles. The next quality improvement should build on that foundation with deliberately shaped edge hardware and distinct material combinations. Multiplying generic speckles or adding continuous animation would weaken the requested industrial identity.
