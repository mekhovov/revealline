# Analog flight transitions: references and art direction

Research checked 2026-09-29. These are visual references and proposed presentation timings, not receiver specifications or a claim of physically simulated RF. No source footage is copied into the game.

## Primary references

- [TBS Fusion product description](https://www.team-blacksheep.com/products/prod:tbs_fusion) explicitly describes colour deterioration and video desynchronization near the reception limit. Its [manufacturer manual](https://www.team-blacksheep.com/tbs-fusion-manual.pdf), pages 9–10, also documents a receiver OSD with channel, scan progress and signal/noise indications. These support colour loss, uncertain picture registration and restrained receiver-style labels.
- [Horizon Hobby / Blade Theory Type W manual](https://www.rc4max.com/pliki/horizon/dokumenty/ZP2951_BLH03055_Manual_US.pdf), printed page 12, associates static/snow with range limits or obstructions and static across channels with an unpowered video transmitter. This is the manufacturer's manual hosted by a retailer; it supports snow as an analog loss cue, not one universal failure sequence.
- [John Seaber's firsthand rapidFIRE power/lock investigation](https://blog.jseaber.com/2018/10/05/solving-unreliable-fat-shark-l1-mod-w-rapidfire/) describes rolling/tearing before video lock and embeds his own recordings: [tearing/no lock](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LO800H-hhJ4) and [transition into low-power trouble](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QMoVpKOZsIU). This is a particular receiver power fault, not ordinary startup timing. The author's description and linked recordings were checked; the videos were not independently inspected frame by frame.
- [Analog Devices AN-1603](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/app-notes/an-1603.html) explains that composite video carries brightness, colour and synchronization, with horizontal sync and colour burst in each line's blanking interval. It supports treating colour, noise and line registration as separate visual controls instead of applying one generic RGB glitch.
- [Analog Devices' engineering response on ghosting](https://ez.analog.com/video/f/q-a/165343/adv7282am-double-image/374293) explains reflected analog-path signals caused by impedance mismatch. A faint offset echo is a useful reference; this particular evidence concerns wired video, so it does not establish that all 5.8 GHz FM multipath produces a clean duplicated picture.
- [HDZero's technology description](https://www.hd-zero.com/technology) identifies its digital transmission errors as isolated pixels. Digital systems do not all fail alike: do not claim that every digital link freezes, or that snowy-looking errors prove a link is analog.

## Four ideas for RevealLine

1. **Acquire the picture on a new flight.** Begin with dim grey snow over the already-masked arena, admit an unstable strip of the actual board, then settle horizontal registration and colour. Suggested total 300–450 ms. The remaining grain disappears once; no repeated reveal/hide cycle. Keep ordinary Pause/Resume direct.
2. **Lose the feed on an actual failure.** Desaturate the visible board, offset one or two horizontal bands, increase monochrome grain, then hold a dark noisy feed briefly before recovery or the existing result view. Suggested total 400–650 ms. Avoid a clean frozen picture as the main loss cue. This is a short visual response to the real failure, not another gameplay delay.
3. **Recover from interference.** Let existing jammer activity drive local grain, modest colour weakening and occasional line displacement. On an actual end/suppression, use one small registration correction and return to clean art. Keep the meaningful field boundary and actors readable. A faint single image echo can be an optional stronger style, not continuous double vision.
4. **A restrained receiver identity.** Briefly pair acquisition with the actual mission name and a small `Acquiring`→`Signal locked` label, or failure with `Signal lost`. The TBS OSD is inspiration for presentation hierarchy, not a reason to invent game measurements or simulate a full equipment menu.

The timings above are design proposals. Use predominantly monochrome, luminance-oriented snow with restrained colour instability. Pixel mosaics, codec-like block breakup and prolonged frame holds evoke digital degradation; tape scratches, VHS head-switching bars, CRT barrel distortion and a collapsing bright dot evoke different equipment. They should not dominate this analog FPV treatment.

All effects should sample only the image already permitted by the game's reveal mask. Keep menus and required controls clear, avoid bright full-frame flashes, and offer a static dark/noisy transition for reduced motion. Present these as presentation changes only; replay inputs, ticks, capture, failure and recovery remain authoritative.
