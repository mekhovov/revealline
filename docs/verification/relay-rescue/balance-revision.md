# Relay Rescue balance revision

Status: independent balance and authoring review complete; final commit/build qualification remains pending. The supplied play feedback has been addressed through an authorized balance revision and retest. The user has authorized us to emulate players and browser-test the revision ourselves; another confirmation is not a prerequisite. The approved mechanics and later phase gates remain in [the plan](../../relay-rescue-plan.md).

## Feedback and baseline

The user's feedback is that both arenas were good, the second was more fun and challenging, and the divider made play unfair and easy because one side could race without challenge. The user prefers the stronghold direction and authorized further emulated and browser testing by the implementation team. This is received play feedback and is the basis for the revision. It does not supply detailed participant, timing, hardware, or mixed-skill measurements; those observations must not be invented.

The frozen `0.46.0-rc.2` Relay Yard has a two-cell SAFE strip spanning all 72 columns. SAFE cells block field-enemy travel, so this strip divides the enemy field into disconnected regions. Its two enemies and single required core leave relatively few objectives and approach threats. The earlier rehearsed route exposed the anchors at about 3.57 seconds and won at about 6.27 seconds without knockdowns. That record proved the stronghold transaction was feasible; it did not prove the level was challenging. First Connection's introductory coverage goal is similarly unsuitable as evidence for the harder stronghold loop.

The correction should give the team several useful decisions about where and when to cut, cover, and recover. It should preserve quick retries, equivalent player tools, legal small or fast cuts, and the ability for a surviving player to finish. A practiced fast clear is not itself a defect. An approach that bypasses the intended encounter without any timing or route decision is the concern.

## Measurable challenge hypotheses

These checks distinguish a stronger encounter from simply more enemies. The implementation review and play receipts must record results rather than silently treating these hypotheses as passed.

| Hypothesis | Evidence to collect | Failure requiring another revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Removing the divider makes both approaches part of a contested playground. | Count connected FIELD regions before play; inspect both spawn-to-objective approaches; replay the former simple opening from both seats. | A remaining SAFE wall partitions an entire uncontested side, or required objectives still fall to the same unopposed opening. |
| Stronghold objectives create several meaningful cuts. | Record anchor exposure and later core defeat per stronghold, new territory per closure, and which approach each player used. Keep core exposure after the whole capture transaction. | One opening banks all required objectives in the same transaction, or the new layout adds traversal without any threat/timing choice. |
| Attack pressure is credible and fair. | For each difficulty, record warning → committed attack → recovery, reachable target points, maximum simultaneous hunters, and complete-encounter gaps. Preserve warning floors: Gentle 1.6s hunter/1.5s emitter; Standard 1.2s/1s; Expert 0.8s/0.8s unless explicitly reviewed. | A hunter warns at a point it cannot reach, attacks across SAFE walls, silently retargets, selects a downed/graced player, or overlapping attackers remove every practical recovery window. |
| Cover improves an otherwise risky cut. | Repeat a threatened route with and without well-timed ally Support; record an intercepted impact, useful slow, completed cut, or prevented knockdown. Compare both seat assignments. | Extra threats only cause unavoidable losses, or Support is still irrelevant because all meaningful cuts finish before threats arrive. |
| Failure remains recoverable after the geometry change. | Recompute the connected SAFE graph and shortest rescue paths. Exercise contact rescue, 2%-since-down recovery, objective recovery, and timeout redeployment while preserving the survivor's trail. | A downed player is isolated, a legitimate rescue route becomes inaccessible, or stronger pressure defeats recovery grace. A long contact route alone is not failure when the intended alternate recovery options remain practical. |
| Difficulty changes preserve choice and trust. | Run the same authored setup on Gentle, Standard, and Expert. Record peak concurrent threats, warnings, knockdowns, reserves, retry seed, and time. | Expert is only faster unavoidable damage; Gentle removes the need to read the encounter; a retry changes its hidden setup. |
| Both players remain active participants. | In emulated routes, verify either seat can cut and provide useful help. In later real paired qualification, record route choices, waiting, confusion/blame, voluntary retries, and each player's useful decisions. | One player reliably waits while the other completes everything, or one must stay in a fixed helper position. Do not enforce equal capture percentages or mandatory role switching. |

The existing rescue analysis measured a longest shortest SAFE path of 106 cells and ideal cooperative traversal of about 10.64 seconds including the channel. Those figures belong to the previous geometry. Recalculate them for the revised arena; connected SAFE ground and average completion time alone do not establish rescue accessibility.

## Independent review record

The first revision-2 working recipes were inspected through public simulation commands, without changing terrain, player positions, enemies, or goals. These are development observations before a frozen commit, not an artifact qualification.

- Both boards have one connected FIELD region and one connected SAFE perimeter of 212 cells. Relay Yard's two separate pillars occupy 16 cells each; its FIELD remains connected around them. The SAFE diameter remains 106, so the previous ideal traversal calculations remain numerically applicable to initial ground. This does not account for reaction time or later captured topology.
- First Connection's straight boosted Join claims 70 cells, or 2.94%, on Gentle and Standard instead of the former 52.94%. Expert's warned Hunters down the two craft at approximately 2.74 and 2.85 seconds; one shared reserve recovers both. The unchanged command does not win any difficulty.
- In Relay Yard, simply boosting inward stops both craft at the pillars and earns no territory or objective progress. No aimed attacks occurred during this 15-second stalled route. It is a non-progressing dead end, not proof of meaningful pressure or an easy win.
- A stronger shortcut was found in the first revision-2 layout and returned to the implementation team: boost inward to approximately columns 14.5/57.5, move up to row 11.5, then Join through the two anchors. They banked at 3.5267 seconds. Spreading along that safe row to columns 33.5/38.5, moving up to row 4.5, and Joining again cleared at **4.5167 seconds on all three difficulties**, with no Support, no knockdowns, and every reserve retained. Pillars delayed Hunter targeting until about 1.7 seconds; the upper core region had no defending enemy after the anchor cut. The recommendation was to revise guarding positions/approaches rather than remove the warning window. This was a rehearsed route, not a claim that a newcomer could execute it immediately.
- The subsequent correction moves the two mirrored drifters into the core region at row 7.5, with opposed horizontal patrols at 2.4 cells/second. Repeating that exact route now exposes the anchors at 3.5267 seconds but downs both craft at **4.025 seconds on all three difficulties**. One shared reserve redeploys them, the exposed core remains undefeated, and the 3.58% banked territory is retained. This addresses the demonstrated shortcut through encounter geography, without shortening warnings. It does not establish that every possible fast route has been exhausted.
- A public-command Standard rescue route verifies recovery on the earned geometry with the real enemies. After the same anchor cut, P0 alone attempts the failed upper approach and is downed at 4.025 seconds. P1 moves along the earned safe row and starts a held-Support contact rescue at 4.1667 seconds; P0 revives at 5.1667 seconds. Both finish safe and stopped, all three reserves remain, neither Support charge is consumed, and the captured territory remains. No state, enemy, terrain, or position was injected for this check.
- That rescue is retained for both player seats in `game/test/coop-live-rescue.test.mjs`; its fresh focused run passed 2/2 cases. Each requires a real trail hit, one contact channel lasting one second, unchanged reserves/charge state/earned territory, and both craft stopped after recovery. The source level remains unchanged.
- The parent also browser-tested Standard with actual controls: a walking upper rush triggered separate P1/P2 warnings, two knockdowns, and reserves changing from 3 to 2 without progress. After retry, smaller upper rectangles banked 4.8% with both craft safe and all reserves. Pauses between route segments allowed inspection; single-operator input and tool latency are not evidence of human reaction-time balance. These early browser observations show dangerous and viable approaches; the separate guard correction above addresses the subsequently discovered boosted shortcut.
- The threat review reports three 100-second clock fixtures covering concurrency budgets 1/2/3, fresh warnings before commits, authored recovery, future deadlines, and repeated turns for every Hunter; this caught and fixed Gentle starvation. The emitter fixture covers its complete warning and three-second rest. These isolate scheduling rules: continuous drifters and already travelling impacts mean they do not establish a globally hazard-free interval.
- The retained `coop-balanced-routes.test.mjs` suite passed 19/19 cases against the corrected recipes. Rehearsed boosted teamwork clears First Connection in 9.825 seconds at 65.38% coverage and Relay Yard in 6.933 seconds at 30.66%, with no knockdowns on all three difficulties. Removing only Support from those exact input logs prevents the First Connection Standard/Expert wins and the Relay Yard Expert win. Gentle and Yard Standard remain completable without Support on these routes; the evidence does not justify claiming Support is compulsory everywhere.
- A walking Yard route uses a 2.5-second wait on freshly earned safe row 12.5 and local cover, then wins in 12.883 seconds without knockdowns on every difficulty. Both moving guards are actually slowed. An Expert variant with a 1.5-second wait wins in 11.883 seconds and intercepts a real travelling impact before the core falls. Every boosted and walking log reproduces the board and normalized full state after exchanging player seats. Small walking corner cuts without Support also remain viable: 4.03% in First Connection and 4.09% in Yard, safely on all three difficulties. These are simulation-informed counterplay proofs, not expected human completion times.
- Independent recipe/builder review found no blocking format issue: explicit template/goal agreement, bounded plain data, compiled-level validation, objective access before/after shields, pack count/byte budgets, independent returned copies, and exclusive output creation are covered. An 80-character level ID previously became an invalid default pack ID when a suffix was appended; the corrected wrapper reuses the ID in the separate pack namespace.
- Browser review exposed an unrelated obstacle defect: after normal inward contact, fresh upward steering stopped at the pillar's first tile seam near row 18. The shared co-op collision helper now ignores harmless corner grazing while retaining inward collision. Seven wall-sliding regressions pass, covering both seats/directions, tile seams, and the shielded core; all 19 balanced routes remain valid. In the actual-control browser retest, both craft slide above the pillar edges and then reach the perimeter, banking 23.0% with both safe, all three reserves, and no objectives completed. The active clock showed about eight seconds, with pauses used for inspection and planning; this is not a human reaction-time measurement.
- Host review fixed stale asynchronous pack reads across Start, return to lobby, and disposal; authored fractional coverage goals, custom instructions, required-core numbering/counts, and lobby keyboard navigation now follow the selected content. Six retained host cases plus four library cases pass. Their minimal DOM/Canvas/RAF doubles exercise real markup and modules, but do not establish physical input or browser-rendering behavior. Frozen-artifact browser checks remain necessary.

The recorded routes now cover a failed rush, safe incremental progress, the repaired core shortcut, successful full runs, meaningful Support, and contact rescue. Remaining technical work is the fresh frozen-source test/build qualification and frozen-artifact browser verification described below. Later human and hardware evidence remains separate.

Once the parent provides the final version and full commit hash, review the related diff and qualify that commit in a clean temporary source export. The existing `0.45.1/qualify.mjs` artifact script already records extraction, committed-blob and executable-mode verification, all four version values, command logs, manifest hashes, distribution checksum, and owned-source cleanup. Its full-test approach is reusable; its version, revision, test list, and expected count must be updated for this revision.

## Evidence limits and later human qualification

Simulation tests establish rules, event ordering, repeatability, and specific route outcomes. A browser operated by one person establishes visible behavior and input integration. Neither establishes mixed-skill teamwork, enjoyment, sustained challenge, or desire to retry. Physical controllers and simultaneous physical touch also require actual hardware checks.

The supplied play feedback has been received and is being acted on; do not mark all human observations as absent or request the same confirmation again. This revision remains open until its implementation review and authorized emulated/browser retests are complete. Record those checks separately from the supplied feedback. The later mixed-skill and Phase 5 paired milestone remain unproven by our retests.

When conducting later paired qualification, teach the three comparison configurations before varying their order on the same arenas: individual cuts with ordinary cover, Joint Cuts with ordinary cover, and full teamwork. Record difficulty, input devices, routes, time, knockdowns, actual helping outcomes, confusion, and whether each player wants another attempt. Broader newcomer/expert claims require their own real participants.
