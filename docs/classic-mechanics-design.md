## Version and integration boundary

Add one closed, versioned rules family: `xonix-level.v4` → `xonix-core.v5` → `xonix-replay.v6` → `fnv1a64-state-v5`, still exactly 72×36 at 120 Hz. Add explicit `xonix-pack.v5` / `xonix-playground.v5` adapters using homogeneous level.v4 campaigns. Keep campaign.v1, the existing class recipe schema, and session.v2 continuation unless their own data needs change. A session envelope is not a substitute for hashing new simulation state.

Do not extend core.v4 in place: capture scoring, failure causes, actor clocks and topology changes alter future states even on familiar looking boards. All v1/v2/v3 levels and replay.v3/v4/v5 keep their original dispatcher, null-input behavior, normalization and checkpoint projection. A featureless v4 level still selects core.v5; absence of classic objects never silently selects an older core. New campaign identity includes the full normalized new level and roster. Old saved contexts remain resolvable by exact identity; same map names do not inherit clears or mastery seals.

Use a finite registry of implemented IDs with exact fields, not scripts, arbitrary callbacks or a plugin interpreter. The implemented seams are `classic-definition.mjs` (bounded descriptors), `classic-motion.mjs` (swept paths), `classic-step.mjs` (ordered transactions), `classic-topology.mjs` / `classic-contour.mjs` (ownership and patrol routes), and `projectClassicState(run)` (checkpoint projection). Only core-owned code mutates a run. Validators own their JSON before reading nested data; unknown fields/IDs, accessors, sparse arrays and mismatched versions are rejected. Renderer and editor consume resolved roles and public events.

P3 soundtrack metadata/binary bundles stay independent; their owner confirmed no reservation of gameplay pack.v5. Pack.v5 is only a gameplay adapter, with `masteries: []` initially and explicit nullable encounter. A separate audio/media manifest may refer to the same campaign without changing its physics. Nonempty new mastery definitions require their own compatible verifier; preserving opaque old awards is not authority to issue new ones.

## Data and bounded state

Retain separate ownership (`FIELD`, `SAFE`, permanent `WALL`) and immutable terrain material masks. Add required `classic: {version: 'classic.v1', terrain: [], powerups: []}`; the empty arrays are valid. Proposed terrain descriptors are exact `{id, kind: 'slow'|'lethal', x,y,w,h}` integer interior rectangles, at most 100, nonoverlapping with each other or walls. Walls remain the existing at-most-100 nonoverlapping rectangles. The editor must split/materialize a painted overlay into canonical disjoint cells/rectangles rather than depend on JSON order.

Retain at most 24 total enemies, 40 objectives and 20 supply pads. Add at most 64 fixed contact powerups, each `{id, kind, x,y}` at a unique non-wall cell center. `kind` is one of `extra-life`, `player-speed`, `enemy-slow`, `enemy-freeze`; no arbitrary strength field. All entity IDs are unique across the level. Keep the 128 KiB level bound, finite number checks, 30-minute replay limit and existing transport limits. Validate maximum combined speed and event work, not just each multiplier separately. New IDs do not raise old limits.

Core state adds a first-claim bitset, cumulative unique-claim count, lives lost, consumed powerup IDs, three pickup expiry ticks, actor clocks, and the registered enemy-specific state. Derived contour graphs can be rebuilt from cells; state influencing route choice (edge, distance, heading, rejoin path, pending activation/warning and next erosion clock) must be checkpointed. Every new descriptor and future-affecting field belongs in the new configuration/board/classic checkpoint sections. Never trust serialized graph caches or event lists as award authority.

| `contour-patrol`              | New route over the current SAFE/FIELD frontier, including internal contours; no fill seed. Swept player and trail contact. No palette, theme or size change selects a behavior. In core.v5, capture uses a registry `seedsField` predicate rather than “every actor except border-patrol.” Only bouncer, eroder, lane-boss and undefeated sentinel seed FIELD; contour patrol and claimed rover do not. Dead/dormant actors never invent hidden anchors. An empty unseeded component fills after a successful closure; a cut with seeded regions on both sides can claim only its new trail.

Keep the sentinel's sole-field-seed constraint. Initial classic encounter maps may add contour/claimed threats but no second bouncer/eroder/lane seed. Its isolation release must use the same new transaction/terminal ordering and exclude failure/redeployment/respawn-completion ticks. This prevents a new role from invalidating the existing finite release guarantee.

### Contour continuity

Build unit half-edges between SAFE and FIELD cells, with canonical row-major cell ID and side order north/east/south/west. Directed edges keep FIELD on the selected side. At a shared vertex follow that side, then straight, opposite turn, reverse; ties use canonical edge ID. This explicitly defines the ambiguous diagonal/checkerboard vertex. Walls and SAFE/SAFE seams are not frontier edges.

After a topology transaction, preserve an actor on a surviving edge with its exact distance and heading. If its edge disappears through capture, enter a visible `rejoining` state: move at normal speed through the SAFE polygon to the nearest reachable frontier vertex. Compute a shortest route on a half-cell lattice whose entire segments lie in closed SAFE cells, exclude wall/FIELD interiors, sort neighbors north/east/south/west, break destination ties by vertex ID. Start with connectors from the actual position; never snap to a cell or teleport across FIELD. Rejoining remains an enemy contact threat. If no frontier is reachable, stay idle until a later topology change creates a route. Rebuild only on a topology revision, not every frame. Protect the current supporting SAFE cell during erosion so the route cannot disappear underneath the actor.

The exact reference branch/rejoin policy is unknown. These rules need corner, split/merge, narrow corridor and enclosed-island fixtures before a contour actor is advertised.

## Terrain is independent of image ownership

| Material       | Player                                                                                                                 | Moving enemies                                              | Capture / erosion                                                                                                         |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| Slow           | While unclaimed, player center inside its half-open cell has speed factor 0.5.                                         | No terrain speed effect.                                    | Counts as normal claimable FIELD. | Lethal         | Swept body contact with active material gives `lethal-terrain` failure, including boundary ties. A successful enclosure can neutralize unoccupied slow/lethal cells without the player crossing them. Artwork cannot change this rule. Pickup and required-objective cells cannot be authored on lethal terrain. Birth positions and respawn/home clearance cannot overlap active lethal material or walls.

Player motion must split at slow-cell boundaries so time spent inside/outside is correct within a fixed tick; testing only the position at tick start is insufficient at high speed. Lethal uses the same swept horizon as enemies/self contact. No tunneling, easing-through-solid-wall or post-capture collision from hidden terrain. Signal fields remain a separate Tactical system, with their existing immunity/ability-lock meanings.

**Known reference difference:** inspected Reloaded footage removes wall artwork after some pocket captures. We choose permanent walls for this first bounded rules edition, matching the approved earlier direction. Do not silently hide a still-solid wall or claim removal parity. A future removable-wall rule needs an explicit type, initial eligibility/coverage definition and support/actor escape tests; it is not smuggled into terrain art.

## Four contact pickups and exact proposed timing

For the first P4 edition pickups have fixed authored positions, visible from the start, radius 0.45 cells and no random spawning, respawn or relocation. Enclosure does not collect them; a swept player contact does. A collected ID stays consumed after life loss, erosion, save/load and replay. Supplies still require their original explicit Pickup action and refill equipment. Use a separate `powerup.collected` event; do not repurpose existing `pickup.collected` supply events or accidentally satisfy equipment mastery with a heart.

All following numbers are proposed tuning defaults compiled into `classic.v1`, not reference measurements:

| Kind           | Effect                                                                                                | Repeated collection                                                              |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `extra-life`   | Add one life, capped at 9; no score substitute at cap. Applies at the successful contact transaction. | Consume once even at cap; event states applied gain 0 or 1.                      |
| `player-speed` | ×1.25 for 600 simulation ticks (5 s).                                                                 | Refresh to `max(oldUntil, activationTick+600)`, never multiply or add durations. |
| `enemy-slow`   | ×0.5 enemy movement for 720 ticks (6 s).                                                              | Same refresh rule with 720; no multiplicative stacking.                          |
| `enemy-freeze` | Zero enemy movement, damage and destructive/attack-clock progression for 360 ticks (3 s).             | Same refresh rule with 360; refresh cannot duplicate rewards.                    |

Timed effects activate at the next fixed tick after contact and use `[activationTick, untilTick)`; an icon says when an effect is pending. This avoids replanning speed halfway through the same contact and makes a same-instant hazard win the tie. A heart cannot rescue a failure at exactly its contact instant. Multiple distinct pickups at the same successful time resolve by ID, without unordered object iteration.

Player effective speed is base × class × signal × terrain × boost × pickup, capped at 60 cells/s for this new edition; expose the cap in rule comparison. Enemy movement uses the minimum active slowdown factor, not their product; freeze overrides all. Local equipment fields retain their class-defined scope (for example the old border-patrol exemption); global classic pickups apply to all registered enemies. Freeze does not make lethal terrain or self-crossing safe. Actor movement/attack/erosion clocks pause during global freeze, preserving remaining warning/active/open durations rather than allowing a missed cycle or immediate overdue attack on thaw. World clock, medal time, player movement, signal zones and player cooldowns continue. Enemy slow changes movement only, not lane warning cadence. This requires new actor clocks; multiplying `vx` alone is insufficient.

Pause/save/load freeze simulation and timed effects exactly. Recovery advances world time and pickup expiry, but clears player-speed immediately on failure or impact redeployment so a fresh post-recovery direction has the normal speed; enemy slow/freeze retain their remaining world duration. Terminal state stops everything. Track `livesLost`, rather than comparing current lives with starting lives, for a clean-run medal: collecting hearts cannot erase a failure or disqualify a genuinely clean clear. Old medal equality stays unchanged on old cores.

## Capture, erosion and anti-farming

Let `E` be the immutable bitset of interior non-wall cells at creation and `N=|E|`. Every slow/lethal cell belongs to E; border and walls do not. `claimedCount=|SAFE∩E|`, `coverage=claimedCount/N`. N never changes during capture, enemy defeat, erosion or the final image reveal.

Keep `everClaimed` and `uniqueClaimedCount`. On a FIELD→SAFE transaction, award cell score only for `newlySafe minus everClaimed`, then union newlySafe into everClaimed. Erosion decreases current claimedCount only; it does not subtract/re-award points. Objectives and collected powerups are one-shot ledgers. No additional score for an eroder hit, contour rejoin, repeated closure or recapturing an erased cell. If a later combo uses area, it must also use first-time cells. The full image after victory never updates these fields.

The first eroder has one pending target cell at a time. At eligible SAFE contact, choose the touched eligible cell with lowest index, reflect its stored velocity and hold its position through a 60-actor-tick visible warning. At expiry reopen that single cell if still eligible, resume movement and begin a 120-actor-tick cooldown, even if blocked. Recontact while pending/cooling cannot accumulate requests. These bounds are deliberately small proposed gameplay values. A requested hole is not traversable before it exists. At most one request per eroder per tick, merged as a sorted unique cell set. Its stopped body remains a contact threat unless globally frozen.

Eligibility is rechecked at commit. Never erase border, wall, unclaimed cells, newly captured cells until the end of that same fixed tick, or the following SAFE protection set:

- Player body-support cells and their one-cell Chebyshev margin; live-cut departure/support cell until closure/failure.
- Active claimed-rover/contour-patrol support cells and their body clearance.
- Captured required-objective cells, hangar/supply cell centers, and a deterministic SAFE path to the permanent border for each such active anchor. Choose shortest four-neighbor path, ties north/east/south/west, when an anchor first becomes SAFE; retain that protected path for this run.

The outer border already protects spawn; its existing grace rule remains. If an anchor has no SAFE route, postpone its protected-path activation until one exists and protect its cell meanwhile; validation/proof must reject a production map whose required route remains inaccessible. A claimed rover still in its activation warning has no support protection: if its required SAFE clearance disappears, cancel the warning, remain dormant and require a new complete warning after clearance returns. The renderer marks protected anchor routes subtly on request, not as unexplained invulnerable territory.

Choose prevention of support erosion, not forced player relocation. Skipped candidates produce an `erosion.blocked` reason and no score; they do not push the actor to another random target. Erosion can split or merge FIELD components but does not trigger an unsolicited flood fill. The next valid closure computes its current seed reachability. Reopened material regains its declared slow/lethal effect only outside the protected support margin. Show black concealment again for reopened cells.

An adversarial player can still make a difficult board, so bounded erosion alone is not a solvability proof. Every eroder production map needs an actual legal win plus deliberately unhelpful captures, reachable mandatory anchors and a demonstrated escape route. No generic guarantee is inferred from flood-fill connectivity alone.

## Deterministic event ordering

Keep exact swept event time within each 1/120 s tick; do not convert all contacts to end-of-frame tests. The v5 branch has this explicit order:

1. At tick start expire global effects, activate prior successful pickup effects, advance unfrozen actor clocks and expose due warning/phase transitions. Resolve valid action/switch edges and equipment fields. Capture the complete pre-movement topology.
2. Plan player/enemy segments to the next event horizon, splitting player speed at material boundaries. Include challenge deadline, self-contact, enemy/trail/lane contact, lethal material, valid closure and contact pickups in the candidate set.
3. Advance all actors and trail only to the earliest horizon. At equal time within EPS, choose failure priority: mission timeout, cut timeout, cable limit, self-contact, lethal terrain, enemy trail, enemy player, boss lane; same-kind ties use stable actor ID. Failure prevents a same-time closure/pickup. Shield absorbs only its declared enemy/lane causes; it still cancels the cut and enters recovery.
4. With no failure, commit closure: secure its FIELD trail, compute four-neighbor retained regions from current eligible seeds, apply ownership/first-claim score and one-shot objectives. Register successful powerup contacts in ID order; enqueue timed effects for next tick. Process earlier contacts at their own horizon rather than denying an earlier pickup because a later failure exists.
5. Apply eroder requests due at this same horizon, in actor-ID order, against the post-capture/protected state. A cell captured anywhere in this tick is protected until the next tick. Rebuild affected topology and repair routes once per transaction; preserve event order `cut.closed` → `cells.claimed` → objective/powerup events → `cells.eroded`/blocked → route changes.
6. Evaluate terminal state after all events at that horizon. Never publish a provisional coverage win before simultaneous erosion. A win is terminal and prevents later movement/erosion. Otherwise plan the remaining subinterval against the updated topology. End-tick due erosion and legitimate sentinel isolation use the same transaction path; failure, redeploy and respawn-completion exclude isolation as today.

This order intentionally defines new v5 ties; old priority code stays byte-compatible. During recovery, enemy motion/clock and expiry continue consistently; no pickup or closure is accepted from the respawning player. A mission deadline may finish recovery as a loss. Host physical input clearing and saved logical continuation are separate from these rules; neither renderer frames nor callback arrival order can change the result.

Core.v5 remains unreleased until its declared registry is complete; later incompatible additions select a new version. No production level may name a planned behavior before its validator, state hash, renderer and demonstration exist.

Required proofs: both turn policies and 60/120 Hz chunking; first/last timed-effect ticks; refresh/slow/freeze interactions and expiry during pause/recovery; contact-before/at/after failure/closure; life cap and clean medal after a lost/replaced life; supplied replay tampering of every new field; save immediately before/after pickup, activation, contour rejoin and erosion; freeze/thaw during warning/active/open and isolation.
