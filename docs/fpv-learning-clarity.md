# FPV learning clarity

The School keeps its existing course identities, physics and recorded commands. This increment makes the conditions for progressing visible, distinguishes lesson chapters from earned progress, and makes recorded full-flight/step demonstrations discoverable even when the player's chosen mode differs from the teaching mode.

## Reported failure: “Acro: the tilt stays”

`beginner-13` is the third primary Acro lesson. Its four real objectives are:

1. Lift into 3.2–4.8 m and remain within the horizontal target, moving no faster than 2.2 m/s and tilted no more than 35°, for one second.
2. Keep a 6–18° tilt while pitch, roll and yaw commands are each within ±5% of centre for 12 ticks (0.24 seconds). Height may be 1–10 m and speed must stay at or below 12 m/s.
3. Counter-steer back to at most 6° tilt, centre the rotation controls and hold for 35 ticks (0.7 seconds).
4. Land softly on the marked pad, lower throttle to at most 10%, and remain settled for 30 ticks (0.6 seconds).

Previously the live objective merely said to remain in a highlighted zone. Later compact-HUD styles hid the detailed instruction, current tilt and missing-condition hint. An objective sound could mark successful takeoff while the next objective required a tilt rather than upright hovering in the same area. Self-level could remain selected, with no matching built-in demonstration exposed by the mode-specific buttons.

The new observer reports each current measurement beside its required value and enlarges only the first unmet condition. Once all conditions qualify, height is the primary measurement; the other readings remain visible. It never awards progress: only a real runtime transition produces the brief completed-step notice. Live input is the applied quantized command, not a speculative radio sample. Speed includes vertical motion; landing tilt is relative to the support surface. Exact input/tilt precision prevents a failed 5.1% input being displayed as a qualifying 5%.

## Navigation, examples and coverage

Named chapter buttons replace bare digits. Their explanation distinguishes preview navigation from automatic objective completion. The coach exposes full-flight and current-step demonstration actions. The host supplies the recorded-mode callback and recommended-mode fallback. Full-flight and section playback use the recorded mode. Return to practice restores the selected practice mode and camera; a nonterminal scored attempt with recorded commands is restored through its command prefix. Empty, completed or unscored practice starts from the lesson launch. Returning remains paused and requires deliberate arming. These host paths require separate browser qualification; the CPU probe below does not exercise them.

All 58 School lessons already have a built-in demonstration in their recommended mode. Alternate-mode examples are not claimed to be bundled by this increment. Advanced rotation, attitude, trajectory and crossing explanations reuse the existing skill-feedback adapter. Ordinary holds and landings now expose height, position, tilt, centred controls, speed, heading, support and throttle requirements where applicable. Gate guidance distinguishes alignment from a real directional crossing. Actor tracking reads runtime reasons and requires both time and target travel; defeat, Contact Hunt and survival summaries use authoritative counters only.

The named chapters and helper are observer-only. They do not change challenge conditions, flight handling, proof identity or scoring. Rendering, responsive layout and demonstration-host integration are qualified separately from this probe.

## Research used for the design

These primary pages were read by the coordinating agent during this review. They are references for interaction design, not claims that another simulator was played or that its assets may be copied.

- [Flight Division](https://www.flightdivision.com/about) supports a structured sequence of small skills. Apply this as one visible current objective, a complete example and repeatable practice, while retaining optional detailed explanations.
- [Xbox objective-clarity guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/109) supports clear goals and revisitable instruction. Apply it through named chapters, current/required values, precise failure hints and meaningful progress announcements.
- [VelociDrone desktop manual](https://www.velocidrone.com/desktop_manual), its [official PDF](https://www.velocidrone.com/downloads/VelociDroneManual.pdf), and the [mobile manual](https://www.velocidrone.com/mobile_manual) provide next-gate indicators and reset/navigation references. Apply a restrained visible/offscreen target cue and fast retry without obscuring the FPV centre. A zone still needs separate altitude and completion-condition guidance.
- [WebFPV](https://webfpv.org/) provides a browser-first reference for explorable maps and gaps. Use coherent accessible scenery, clear flight lines and quick entry as design goals. The page alone does not establish performance, physics fidelity or redistribution rights.
- [Liftoff support](https://www.liftoff-game.com/support), [Milestone 0.6.0](https://www.liftoff-game.com/news/milestone-060-released), and [campaign announcement](https://www.liftoff-game.com/news/campaign-mode-announced) inform structured progression, discoverable guidance and challenge collections. Retain open challenge access and verified replay examples rather than adding unlock friction.

The Virtual Mentor article did not load in this review and is not supporting evidence. English/Ukrainian Zaruba searches did not identify a verifiable simulator; no inspection or feature claim is made for it. No competitor models, textures, audio or game data are imported by this increment.

## Reproducible functional evidence

Run from the repository root:

```sh
node docs/evidence/fpv-learning-clarity-probe.mjs
```

The probe writes `docs/fpv-learning-clarity-verification.json`, recording its own SHA-256, source hashes, base commit and every lesson's proof digest, real step-transition ticks and terminal identity. It checks unchanged physics/proof bytes against the base commit, executes ordinary recorded commands, then independently calls the production replay verifier for each complete proof.

Latest recorded result:

- 58 recommended-mode examples complete all their actual objectives, including all four `beginner-13` objectives; every final identity matches its existing proof.
- 87,518 direct simulation ticks plus 87,518 independent replay ticks.
- 56,796 hold/landing frame comparisons and 239 accepted hold/landing transitions agree with runtime criteria, with zero mismatches.
- 1,496 localized objective evaluations cover every School step in both flight modes and EN/UK, including missing initial state.
- 35 named checks cover centred-input and tilt boundaries, vertical speed, zone boundaries, directional gate approach, lateral misses, null/stale actor tracking, target travel, unordered contact targets and missing-state behaviour.

This is manual functional qualification, not additional unit-test coverage. It does not establish browser presentation, first-time-player comprehension, physical-radio acceptance, mobile/Steam Deck performance or public deployment. Those outcomes must be recorded separately; source hashes make any later changes distinguishable from this receipt.

The probe does not load the renderer or run host navigation. Its null-state and boundary fixtures are deterministic observer checks; they are not a substitute for the 58 independently replayed lesson proofs. It does not qualify target destruction, larger world revisions, random selection or shared audio. It also does not exercise the 28 optional alternate School demonstrations, optional imported worlds, the legacy Academy host, dropped-frame recovery on physical hardware or performance under production art loads.
