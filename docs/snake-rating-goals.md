# Snake result rating goals

F23c adds a short explanation beside the accepted current-attempt stars in the
existing Snake result summary. The briefing retains its existing exact calibrated
thresholds and explicit completion-only policies. No separate dialog, calibration,
record schema or new award owner is introduced.

A verified calibrated mission completion shows the current attempt's move count,
the next star target and the exact integer reduction needed on a retry. Three stars
identify the highest rating. The result reads the native grade already calculated
from the current run; it never substitutes the historical fewest-moves record.
English and Ukrainian refresh through the existing locale owner, including singular
and plural move counts. Unchanged frames do not replace the explanation repeatedly.

Unaccepted or incomplete attempts, imported recordings, competitive/endless games,
variable-condition missions and uncalibrated setups promise no higher rating. A
verified session-only result may explain its grade while retaining the existing
saving-failure notice. Retry clears the old explanation and uses its original
mission/start owner. Existing briefing criteria remain available without change.

Native tests complete the original Open Loop route and legal pre-route circuits,
verify the resulting recordings, submit them to the real record admission/store,
and retain an earlier Gold best while explaining later Silver and Bronze attempts.
Boundary checks retain exact integer comparisons and explicit unavailable cases.
Actual host-handler tests earn the same three outcomes through keyboard inputs,
wait for accepted completion, switch EN/UK, retain replay/storage/focused Retry,
and exercise the real Retry and import controls. The host harness models canvas,
network and record-receipt services; real native admission is qualified separately.

Actual browser review must check the result summary at compact width, reach the
complete explanation, and keep earned play separate from any staged presentation
fixture. These source tests do not establish browser layout, human comprehension,
assistive-technology or physical-device acceptance.
