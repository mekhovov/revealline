## Shared visual grammar

Use premium photographic workshop dioramas with believable materials and proportions: **at least one immediately recognizable carbon-frame FPV quad in every image**. Four separate motor positions, slender carbon arms, a small front camera, simple propeller silhouettes and a compact centre body must read at thumbnail size. Avoid a generic camera-drone shell, six rotors, impossible melted frames or delicate jewellery. In close scenes, goggles, motor trays, soldering irons in stands, silicone mats, drivers and hard cases are the supporting cast. No fabricated component labels, specifications, readable circuitry, wiring diagrams, step sequences or flight/targeting instructions.

Show a coherent fictional workshop and donation effort supporting Ukraine. No battle scene, weapon payload, tactical screen, military target, real unit identifier, deployment route or claimed completed delivery. Do not depict real participants or identifiable veterans from photographs. Keep scenes human in purpose through worn tools, accessible tables and shared workspaces, with no generated people required. These limits preserve an illustrative game; they do not erase the organisation's stated defence-support purpose.

All compositions are opaque 2:1. Prefer eye-catching grounded equipment, textured timber, matte violet cabinets, restrained yellow lamps and blue/yellow fabric over palatial gardens, porcelain villages, mystical glowing cubes or abstract trinkets. Use one clear focal subject per image; keep its propeller tips inside the safe crop. Do not paint game actors, trails, objective symbols, gate arrows, interface labels, logos or watermarks into a reveal image. Runtime chrome and official assets supply those elements.

## Production and acceptance boundary

Start with three representative replacements: Workshop Lights 01 (quad/workshop recognition), Parts in Motion 03 (FPV goggles and carbon camera craft), and The Careful Handoff 01 (Ukraine-support donation cases). Review them together for recognition, material realism and gameplay contrast before propagating the style. Subsequent batches remain at most three images, with one generation per mission and a separate original/selected inspection record. Do not register an image just because it is violet and yellow.

Use the existing candidate provenance/import workflow: exact generated master outside the player packet, prompt and source-reference URLs recorded, an inspected opaque 2:1 selected derivative at most 1 MiB, exact hashes/dimensions and native resize command, explicit completed original/selected inspection states, human artwork approval pending until obtained. A mechanical resize cannot repair a wrong silhouette or misleading scene. Preserve existing published image bytes and capture affected prior presentations before replacing references; art changes can change generated execution identities even without geometry changes.

Acceptance needs three separate observations: (1) equipment reads as FPV and the story reads as DroneAid support for Ukraine; (2) the exact selected image preserves current return/objective/gate/field cues in the game, including partial reveal and reduced motion; (3) the final content/rights review is recorded without treating a generation receipt or previous formative playtest as human approval of this replacement batch. The current document establishes none of those approvals in advance.
