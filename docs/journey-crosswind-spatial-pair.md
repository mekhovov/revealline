# Crosswind spatial pair

Status: candidate successor stacked on the first ornament spatial pair. Balance
review pending; no default route or historical edition changes in this batch.

## Design boundary

“Survey markers” uses original component-like baffles and receiver-pad geometry
to evoke an inactive FPV workbench without copying a particular device. “Windbreak
weave” uses an original rectilinear abstraction of the Ukrainian bezkonechnyk,
the continuous meander/endless-line motif. The Encyclopedia of Ukraine documents
the meander among dominant central-Ukrainian pysanka elements and identifies
meandering ornament in Ukrainian archaeological material:

- https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CE%5CA%5CEasteregg.htm
- https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CM%5CI%5CMizynarcheologicalsite.htm

These are compositional references, not reproduced artifacts or claims about one
regional pattern. Reveal Line’s established wall, foundation, enemy and
directional-field meanings remain visually and mechanically authoritative.

## Survey markers

Distinctive decision: take the short marked receiver corridor while its central
keeper is exposed, or dogleg around an antenna-like baffle toward a side work pad.

Two intended approaches:

1. **Direct receiver:** leave the central work pad through a vertical marked
   corridor, close quickly on the north receiver, then use it to approach a side
   baffle from above.
2. **Component dogleg:** depart horizontally, connect a side work pad, then circle
   a staggered baffle end while the perimeter patrol is elsewhere.

Three keepers retain the north-west, workbench and south-east approaches. The
perimeter patrol pressures the long outer alternative. Capturing a directional
field neutralizes it and establishes a return; it does not chain-capture another
receiver pad.

## Windbreak weave

Distinctive decision: take a short marked thread through the central opening, or
turn around alternating wall ends to reshape the frontier before connecting an
outer landing.

Two intended approaches:

1. **Thread first:** close north through the central opening, then alternate sides
   of the stepped meander to avoid repeating one exposed line.
2. **Weave first:** connect a side landing, follow the frontier movement, then use
   the opposite wall end for a longer enclosure toward the south landing.

Two keepers retain opposite sides while the frontier patrol follows the changing
capture contour. The broken meander never creates a sealed remote chamber, and a
wall never substitutes for a return surface.
