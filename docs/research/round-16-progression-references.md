## Recommended playable increment

### 1. One visible, optional equipment goal per Homeward picture

Keep the mastery plan's **Steady Signal**, **Supply Line** and **Safe Return** definitions. The useful extension is an intelligible relationship between an action and a seal, not another general “play ten times” counter.

Before launch, show one short optional goal with the required mission objective above it. During a pause, show the complete criterion and concrete state. In particular, Supply Line contains several steps: a player should be able to see which pad, suppressed region or hangar remains, without receiving a large constantly updating checklist over the live cut. Its complexity warrants a teaching/readability check before adding further clauses.

Use honest criterion wording. A clean impact recovery means the recorded facts show an eligible pulse and a clean win; it does not establish that the pulse was the only possible way to survive. A failed cut must not quietly count as a completed resistant route. The existing mastery plan already specifies replay verification, one-attempt conjunctions and identity/version preservation; this research does not replace those contracts with progress bars assembled from arbitrary UI events.

On completion, show the full picture regardless of mastery. Add one small named seal only after verification, with its saved setup in details. A missed goal is **“Optional goal not earned this run”**, followed by the remaining condition if requested—not a second failure screen. Let the player choose **Continue**, **View picture**, **Retry for this goal**, or a calm return. An optional check still running must not hold the picture or next-map action hostage. These are our concrete UI choices, informed by the Hades record/goal example above.

### 2. A new two-stage board with a response every class can perform

Retain the separate Sentinel Relay map instead of appending a surprise mandatory boss to a previously completed chapter. Stage 1 asks the player to claim a shield relay. Stage 2 asks for a cut closure during the core's recovery opening after a different lane attack. That changes the decision, while the four-direction capture vocabulary remains familiar.

Use a stable three-part cue at every phase: **shape/state**, **lane footprint**, and **short instruction**. For example, a closed ring and “Shield relay first”; then a striped lane with “Lane charging”; finally an open diamond with “Core open · close a cut.” Sound may reinforce the transition. A skin, particle fade or audio completion cannot be authoritative for damage or vulnerability.

The planned 1.5-second warning and two-second recovery are initial fictional tuning values, not research-derived accessibility thresholds. First prove the phase logic and safe routes, then check whether newcomers can understand and act in the window. If the window is consistently misunderstood, change the cue or teaching arrangement before simply lengthening every timer. If a clearly understood window still excludes players, a separately identified relaxed rules variant is a reasonable follow-up. Do not silently change the active recorded run's rules.

The ordinary solution must work without shield, stun or impact. A class-specific opportunity may interrupt a threat or make a route easier, but cannot erase the phase objective or secretly extend the opening. This applies Furi's familiar-action sequence and Riot's broadly available response principle to territory capture; neither source specifies this boss's mechanics.

### 3. A result that feels complete and allows leaving

Keep the existing four world finales and preserved original artwork. Put the badge accent within the existing presentation budget; do not stack another compulsory animation after the picture. The FPV result can settle into its cleared scene; Atlas into a still stitch border; retro into a stopped cabinet-like picture; the business world into calm connected savings marks. These are original presentation proposals, not claims about the source games or real-world events.

The clear picture is a stable destination: no countdown, auto-next launch, animated pressure to retry, or loss for leaving. A **Return to collection** or **Done for now** action should state where it goes and leave the completed scene available later. Display the actual local save state; a session-only result must not be described as safely persisted. This uses the existing continuity and export contracts rather than inventing cloud saving.

For the chapter's final clear, show the completed set before outstanding seals. Optional pursuit is an invitation attached to a named picture and route. Do not convert it into streaks, expiring rewards, randomized drops, reward decay, compulsory replay counts or a forced harder setting. Whether players choose another attempt is a playtest question, not something these references guarantee.

## Controller-settings recommendations connected to this increment

The current complete-draft editor already supports button maps, selected label families, independent flight/menu axes/inversions and Press/Release values. Preserve its Apply/Cancel/default behavior and text-only labels. The newly corrected explicit control names also let the real controller preview explain “Use ability button” rather than a generated element ID. [Settings contract](../controller-settings.md).

The next useful small addition would be an **optional input preview inside practice**, showing the chosen context, normalized axis point and current thresholds. It must display only an already sampled snapshot and must not navigate the menu or trigger flight from a draft. Label it **Draft preview** versus **Applied controls**. The current practice bridge has four simulated axes, allowing both configured stick pairs to be exercised, but those synthetic values do not demonstrate physical right-stick mapping or real jitter. The XAG 114 preview guidance supports this recommendation, but does not prescribe our threshold algorithm.

For the new encounter and all goal/results menus, keep a discrete-button route: no hold-to-read objective, mandatory simultaneous chord or unskippable repeated-button tally. Preserve explicit release after remapping or scope change. Remapping and reduced effects must not change the score/rules identity merely because labels or presentation differ; a genuine relaxed boss schedule is a different rules choice. Physical controller testing remains separate from practice simulation.

Use a few consented, short sessions to learn whether people understand a lost cut, see the picture reward, can decline mastery and choose what to do next. Record observable mistakes and explanations before asking about enjoyment. A tiny sample can expose a confusing cue; it cannot establish a retention rate, diagnose compulsive play or justify an “addictive” claim.

Implementation order remains the existing plan's: freeze compatibility facts, add the pure optional observer/verified awards and result display, then introduce the opt-in staged boss with its versioned replay path. The controller diagnostic preview can follow independently. No source, pack, art, skill or release artifact is changed by this research document.
