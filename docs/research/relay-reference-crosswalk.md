# Relay Labyrinth — original adaptation candidates

These are greybox proposals, not final dispositions or released missions. The
original observation ledger, file hashes and limitations remain unchanged.
Static screenshots establish spatial motifs, not collision, timing, enemy AI or
capture semantics. No coordinates, artwork or names are copied.

| Reference | Original candidate | Retained spatial idea | Deliberate redesign |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pack 2, level 6 | Three compounds | Connected groups around a hub | Asymmetric yards with two independent shortcuts, an upper fallback and an escape corridor for an activated reclaimed-ground roamer. |
| Pack 2, level 9 | Spiral stores | Paired inward-turning structures | Broad open sides, offset breaks and a central spine. Choose relay order instead of tracing every bend. |
| Pack 3, level 11 | Nested relays | Layered bands and openings | A conspicuous side exit remains open; two numbered connectors shorten different returns. Field enemies, not gates, retain regions. |
| Pack 4, level 9 | Watchpost exchange | Hub and repeated compartments | Four occupied chambers with two cross-hub connections, not eight mandatory similar rooms. Keep only if route evidence and human comparison distinguish it from Three compounds. |

The new First link and Second approach teach and practice the one new rule before
the first reference combination. Switchback exchange is an optional known-rule
Remix. Core bands are 9–10, following Livewire's band 9 finish without resetting
player handling, enemy tiers or lives. No countdown or random-drop dependency is
introduced. All first-return checks capture a visible trigger and open a gate.

## Design changes from executable probes

The initial Standard/immediate/seed-1 greedy search found legal no-loss clears for
all seven layouts, but did not establish their quality. Its rejected route outputs
are retained as local probes, not the eventual committed qualification fixture.

- Second approach's eastern connector opened only on the winning cut. Move its
  trigger onto the approach from the middle platform so opening can affect later
  play; retain an alternative outside approach.
- Spiral stores reached 70.7% on its second cut and 84.9% on its third. Both
  keepers could leave most exterior field unoccupied. Add an ordinary measured
  keeper outside the structures; do not raise global speed or add a timer.
- Nested relays initially enclosed an outer ring. Keep a wide eastern break so
  the final large exterior capture need not become a separate low-interest task.
- The Remix's eastern connector also opened only on the final cut. Move its
  trigger to a nearby established return approach. Later route checks must prove
  that opened ground is useful before completion, not merely a victory effect.

These are hypotheses under test. A solver's short clear time is not a human
duration estimate. Alternate orders, all presets/steering, seeds, delay tolerance,
quota cleanup, optional goals, contrast, native play and human pacing remain gates.

## Primary-source recheck, 20 September 2026

The [Fortix manual, tutorial page](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/45400/manuals/fortix_PC_manual_WEB.pdf?t=1447353028)
teaches enclosure before captured markers activate catapults against towers.
Our inference is to make a captured relay change the next route decision; we do
not import Fortix's combat, timer or enemy-removal rules.

The [AirXonix developer rules](https://www.axysoft.com/airxonix/) distinguish balls
in the field from mines on filled territory. Our inference is that an opened
connector is reclaimed ground, not a promise of immunity from ground threats.
Neither source demonstrates that these features guarantee popularity or retention.
