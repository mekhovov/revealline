# Border cultural routes · v12 source note

This source-ready successor changes exactly `second-landing`, `long-rail`, and
`new-frontier`. The registered `whole-spatial-v12` route receives a separate
profile and suspended-run key. Its bounded mission-library adapter retains the
exact compiled v11 missions as manual-only Previous Journey cards; v10 and v9
historical cards remain available as well. Authored current progression skips
all prior-edition cards.

No release, production asset, package version, milestone, tag, or Pages state is
owned by this change.

## Attribution and adaptation boundary

These records are evidence for regional medium and large-scale compositional
rhythm. They are not coordinate sources and do not grant permission to copy an
object, chart, painting, palette, stitch pattern, figure, or symbol.

- `second-landing` studies Reshetylivka white-on-white embroidery from Poltava
  region. The Regional Centre under the auspices of UNESCO records the element
  on Ukraine's National ICH Register and describes geometric/floral merezhka,
  cutwork with square openings, and light/dark relief:
  <https://www.unesco-centerbg.org/en/2021/11/22/white-on-white-technique-of-embroidery-of-reshetylivka/>.
  The game uses an original high-contrast open aperture and separated far band;
  it does not imitate a white-on-white palette or a stitch chart.
- `long-rail` studies Petrykivka decorative painting from Dnipropetrovsk region,
  UNESCO Representative List nomination `00893`:
  <https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/petrykivka-decorative-painting-as-a-phenomenon-of-the-ukrainian-ornamental-folk-art-00893>.
  The supporting Ukrainian inventory records branch and frieze compositions:
  <https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/18937-EN.pdf>. The game uses an original
  rectilinear stem with separated branch masses. Petrykivka is painting, not
  embroidery, and no flower or painted object is traced.
- `new-frontier` studies the Kosiv painted-ceramics tradition, UNESCO
  Representative List nomination `01456`:
  <https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/tradition-of-kosiv-painted-ceramics-01456>.
  UNESCO records graphical contour work on local clay, painted tiles and other
  objects, and figurative ornament grounded in Hutsul community life and local
  flora and fauna. The game uses an original bilateral open-frame rhythm only.
  It does not reproduce pottery figures or turn people, animals, or
  meaning-bearing motifs into targets. This ceramic study is not the Hutsul
  garment-construction study used by v11.
