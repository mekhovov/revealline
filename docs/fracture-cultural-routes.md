## Cultural source boundaries

- [UNESCO's Örnek record](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ornek-a-crimean-tatar-ornament-and-knowledge-about-it-01601) describes Crimean Tatar ornamental knowledge and geometric groupings used in weaving. First fracture borrows broad offset grouping only. It does not copy an ornament, symbol, narrative meaning, palette, object or source coordinates.
- The [Ivan Honchar Museum's Borshchiv embroidery article](https://old.honchar.org.ua/p/vidkryly-sekrety-borschivskoji-vyshyvky/) describes multiple techniques and dense relief with regional variation. Two districts borrows dense-versus-open band contrast only. It does not copy a shirt, sleeve, stitch, village pattern, palette, legend, meaning or source coordinates.
- The [National Museum-Preserve of Ukrainian Pottery's Krychevsky panel article](https://opishne-museum.gov.ua/ornamenty-yaki-mozhna-vidchuty/) describes openwork diagonal panels with varied proportions and tactile access. Fracture remix borrows open-panel spacing and varied proportions only. It does not copy an ornament, panel outline, artwork, tactile claim or source coordinates.

These sources support cultural context and broad compositional vocabulary. They do not establish gameplay balance, permission to copy an object or universal symbolic meanings.
