## Scope and continuity

This bounded expansion follows the user's approved spatial-redesign plan and

The intended successor is the explicit, opt-in `whole-ornament-v2` study route.
The default experience remains unchanged. Route integration, Studio visibility,

Revise nine existing missions copy-on-write, preserving their original mission
references, earlier playable editions and progression owners. Do not remove old
levels or erase settings, installed content, uploads or Studio projects. Keep the
first three ornament studies intact; this batch is nine additional adaptations,
not a claim that all nine existing maps are already redesigned. Explicit study
selection must launch the selected edition directly rather than another picker.

Keep shared gp4 physics, difficulty preparation, continuous/immediate steering
and Grid + Buffer. Ordinary field keepers remain straight between physical
impacts. Walls block movement and do not close cuts; reclaimed foundations close
cuts, remain non-scoring and retain their existing protection. Preserve the
four-connected enemy-seeded capture contract. Do not introduce per-map speed
exceptions, surprise mid-field turns, new enemy systems or compulsory timers to
make a decorative layout feel difficult.

## How the sources are used

Eleven museum/community entries document motif families beyond diamonds,
zigzag bands and pysanka. Three more document regional shirt construction and
stitch placement, not newly identified motif names.

The map proposals are original gameplay abstractions. No museum photograph,
embroidery chart, artist's drawing or published composition is copied or traced.
A traditional technique or broad motif family does not make a particular modern
artist's design free to reproduce. Preserve source attribution and review asset
licensing separately if any actual source media is later proposed for use.

Embroidery, woven rushnyky, kilims, decorative painting, ceramic tiles, carved
wood and paper-cut traditions are distinct. Do not call them all vyshyvanka.
Place, date, technique and object-level caveats take precedence over generic
symbolism claims. A catalogue's interpretation of one composition is not a
universal dictionary of Ukrainian signs.

Source observations and original gameplay proposals are distinguished below.

## Source atlas

### U01 — Podillian stars and interstitial motifs

Source: [Honchar Museum, rushnyk КН-22884](https://honchar.org.ua/collections/detail/1769).
Vilshanka, Podillia, first half of the twentieth century. The catalogue describes
large multicoloured eight-point stars, with smaller stars and diamonds between
them, placed symmetrically at the towel ends.

Design translation: open star-shaped obstacle clusters with different entrance
axes; smaller detached masses interrupt a trivial full-width cut. The player
chooses a central connection or a longer shoulder enclosure.

Caveat: this is a particular embroidered rushnyk, not proof that every star has
one meaning or that this arrangement represents every Podillian village. Do not
copy its stitch chart. Preserve generous passages rather than eight tiny slaloms.

### U02 — Woven kilim: six-petal flowers and flowering branches

Source: [National Museum of Decorative Art of Ukraine, kilim](https://museum.mincult.gov.ua/collections/kilim-62650).
The collection record describes a handwoven wool kilim with alternating plain
and ornamented bands; its ornament alternates six-petal flowers and flowering
branches within a symmetrical composition.

Design translation: alternate compact flower-shaped obstacle masses with longer
branching ones. These produce differently sized capture opportunities and
different physical rebound paths, not merely differently coloured rooms.

Caveat: the record consulted does not establish a creation date or regional
origin. Do not label the map as a historical reconstruction of a named region.
Kilim weaving is not shirt embroidery.

### U03 — Krolevets-style architectural and three-candle bands

Source: [Honchar Museum, woven rushnyk КН-1874](https://honchar.org.ua/collections/detail/1712).
The early-twentieth-century object has a symmetrical banded composition with
schematic churches, three-candle holders and geometric ornament. The narrative
description connects it with Chornobai district, Cherkasy region, while the
structured creation-location fields are incomplete.

Design translation: original three-pronged obstacle clusters create unequal
side bays, a central approach and an open outer bypass.

Caveat: distinguish recorded provenance from uncertainty. Religious imagery is
contextual, not a destruction target; a geometric abstraction should not be
presented as an authentic sacred composition. Not selected for the nine-map batch.

### U04 — Krolevets central flower with ducks

Source: [Museum of Krolevets Weaving, 2001 woven rushnyk](https://museum.mincult.gov.ua/collections/rushnik-tkaniy-kroleveckiy-42024).
The record credits V. H. Savytska and V. O. Myronenko and describes a central
flower with ducks beneath it, woven bands and an anniversary inscription.

Design translation: a central branching obstacle and two separated bird-like
wings create a direct central passage versus outside enclosures.

Caveat: this is a modern authored commemorative work, not an anonymous ancient
chart. Do not reproduce its exact layout or inscription, or universalize the
catalogue's named flower interpretation. Applying this family to a mission named
Dnipro crossings does not establish a historical Dnipro provenance.

### U05 — Reshetylivka square cutwork and merezhka

Source: [UNESCO-affiliated Regional Centre Sofia, Reshetylivka white-on-white embroidery](https://www.unesco-centerbg.org/en/2021/11/22/white-on-white-technique-of-embroidery-of-reshetylivka/).
The account documents counted satin stitch, hemstitch and cutwork with one,
three, five or more square openings. Several techniques can coexist in one work.

Design translation: open square brackets and discontinuous lattice sections,
with broad connections and explicitly separate reclaimed return ground. Securing
one connection should change the next route decision.

Caveat: this source identifies Ukrainian national intangible-heritage status;
it does not establish UNESCO Representative List inscription. White-on-white
relief belongs in presentation, not low-contrast collision or hazard graphics.

### U06 — Poltavian branch, oak-leaf and broken-tree arrangements

Source: [Nina Andriichuk, National History Museum collection study](https://museum.kh.ua/academic/publications.html?n=929),
published by the Kharkiv Historical Museum. The study records branch, oak-leaf,
rose, broken-tree and kalyna motifs, with vertical or staggered sleeve layouts.
It also explains incomplete provenance in parts of the collection.

Design translation: offset branching obstacle masses create small leaf-side
pockets and a longer inter-branch crossing. Capturing an approach should simplify
later exposure rather than leave unrelated quota cleanup.

Caveat: do not assign every motif to one village, date or universal meaning.
Use an original geometric branch family, not a facsimile of a museum shirt.

### U07 — Petrykivka noncrossing botanical compositions

Source: [Ukraine's heritage inventory submitted to UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/18937-EN.pdf?v=1361095193).
The inventory identifies bouquet, branch, flowerpot and frieze compositions,
fantastic flowers and feather-like foliage. It describes stems and branches that
do not cross one another.

Design translation: two or three separated botanical wall masses create clear
negative-space routes. A branch-side capture can establish a return before the
longer central crossing; a different route can secure the outer space first.

Caveat: Petrykivka is decorative painting, not a synonym for embroidery. Preserve
composition principles, not a contemporary painter's brushwork. Fine foliage
belongs in art rather than dense single-cell collision detail.

### U08 — Kosiv paired-animal tile panel

Source: [Iryna Beketova's museum attribution study, printed page 155](https://ceramology-inst.gov.ua/app/uploads/2025/05/ceramology2007_3_2.pdf#page=155).
The publication records the nineteenth-century Kosiv tile titled Lions,
К-13489 / Е-24765/16, in the National Museum of Ukrainian Folk Decorative Art
collection. It provides object attribution and an illustrated paired-animal panel.

Design translation: two opposing stepped obstacle masses frame a usable central
passage. Different flank captures should change pressure and return options.

Caveat: this is ceramic panel composition, not vyshyvanka. Borrow bilateral
organization without tracing the tile or inventing symbolic meaning. The linked
PDF page number refers to the document's printed page, not a copied game asset.

### U09 — Crimean Tatar Örnek: nested tulip and rose

Source: [ALЄM community account of Örnek](https://www.ornek-crimea.com/about-ornek-uk).
The community describes meaningful combinations of plant symbols and explicitly
explains a tulip within a rose as a relationship or marriage composition within
Örnek. Compositions are understood and created by practitioners and community
members, not interchangeable decorative tokens.

Design translation: potentially nested, deliberately open floral enclosures
with inner-first and outer-first capture decisions.

Caveat: credit Crimean Tatar heritage of Ukraine, not generic ethnic-Ukrainian
embroidery. Changing the arrangement can change its meaning. This remains a
**later practitioner-review candidate**, excluded from this nine-map geometry
set. No consultation, endorsement or cultural approval has been obtained.

### U10 — Carved concentric centre, border and alternating wheat elements

Source: [Bukovynian Centre of Culture and Arts on Volodymyr Voronchak's Christmas plate](https://www.bukcentre.cv.ua/index.php/nasha-robota/onlain-etnoproiekt-bukovynske-rizdvo/5105-znakove-pysmo-rizdvianoi-tareli-volodymyra-voronchaka.html).
The 1999 pearwood work combines dry carving and metal inlay. The account describes
a central star/sun, repeated elements alternating with wheat motifs and a broad
ornamented rim.

Design translation: original broken concentric wall arcs with alternating wide
openings create an outer rotation versus a centre-entry decision.

Caveat: this is a named modern artist's Christmas composition. Borrow centre/rim
rhythm without reproducing the plate or presenting its motifs as universally
applicable historical signs. Open arcs are a gameplay adaptation, not a claim
that the source plate has interrupted rings.

### U11 — Vytynanka vase-and-bird negative space

Source: [Khmelnytskyi Regional Art Museum, Volodymyr Hagenmeister's Vase with Birds](https://xoxm.art/creation/vytynanka-vazon-z-ptakhamy/).
The collection record dates the work to 1930 and identifies the held medium as
paper and lithography.

Design translation: a branching central obstacle and separated lateral masses
make negative space the playable structure. One approach secures an exterior
return; another enters between branches before enclosing a larger area.

Caveat: do not call this held museum object an original cut-paper sheet: its
catalogued medium is lithography. Do not copy the artist's composition. The
gameplay openings and capture decisions are new, not museum-authenticated.

### U12 — Verkhovyna Hutsul shoulder/seam construction

Source: [Honchar Museum, shirt КН-444 construction record](https://honchar.org.ua/to-learn/sorochka-zhinocha-chernivetska-oblast-poch-hh-st-1-i139).
The early-twentieth-century Verkhovyna shirt uses separate embroidered details
applied at the shoulder-sleeve join, with techniques including Hutsul braid and
nyz. The title and metadata identify Ivano-Frankivsk/Verkhovyna despite the URL's
misleading Chernivtsi words.

Design translation: a potential strong joining axis and offset paired obstacle
groups, making connection order meaningful.

Caveat: this documents construction and technique, not a newly named motif or a
claim that the proposed map reproduces the shirt. The contemporary technical
pattern has credited authors; it must not be copied. Not selected in this batch.

### U13 — Vyzhenka braided join and double-prutyk seam

Source: [Honchar Museum, shirt КН-26139 construction record](https://honchar.org.ua/to-learn/sorochka-zhinocha-chernivetska-oblast-poch-hh-st-i134).
The early-twentieth-century shirt from Vyzhenka documents a braided joining seam
and double-prutyk treatment. Its geographical location is Chernivtsi region;
the museum's ethnographic classification is Hutsul.

Design translation: potential paired discontinuous obstacle lines with
alternating wide entrances and a separately marked reclaimed return path.

Caveat: administrative geography does not define a uniform Bukovynian motif.
These are construction/technique observations, not proof of a particular
historical map-like pattern. Do not copy the credited technical chart. Not
selected in this batch.

### U14 — Western Polissia counted stitch and horizontal joining

Source: [Honchar Museum, shirt КН-16415 construction record](https://honchar.org.ua/to-learn/sorochka-zhinocha-chernivetska-oblast-poch-hh-st-1-1-1-i147).
The Kysorychi shirt, first half of the twentieth century, documents zanyzuvannia,
stem stitch and horizontal shoulder joins.

Design translation: potential short repeated obstacle groups interrupted by
larger passages, supporting a measured choice between incremental and ambitious
captures.

Caveat: technique and construction are established, but our proposed grid layout
is not an authenticated regional chart. Keep modern chart authorship separate
from historical object authorship. Not selected in this batch.

## Planned nine-map mapping

Every mapping is pending implementation and checks. Retain each original picture
as a study placeholder, not a claimed new cultural background. Final composition,
licensing, readability and cultural fit remain separate gates. Do not imply that
an existing picture depicts the cited craft.

- `four-motor-landings` → U01 Podillian stars: enter a cluster or establish a
  shoulder return; preserve meaningful disconnected landing choices.
- `circuit-lanes` → U05 Reshetylivka cutwork: secure a lattice connection first
  or enclose an outside section through broad passages.
- `twin-lens-chambers` → U08 Kosiv paired panels: choose opposing flanks or the
  central opening; captures must affect later pressure.
- `toolbench-weave` → U06 Poltavian branches: capture a short leaf-side pocket
  or connect across offset branch gaps.
- `dnipro-crossings` → U04 Krolevets flower/bird: use the central passage
  between masses or establish an exterior return.
- `two-districts` → U02 Kilim flower/branch alternation: choose compact
  enclosures or a longer branch-side cut across unequal spaces.
- `two-ways-home` → U07 Petrykivka branches: establish one of two readable
  returns around nonintersecting botanical masses.
- `second-approach` → U11 Vytynanka negative space: secure an outside return
  first or enter between branches before the larger capture.
- `windbreak-weave` → U10 Carved centre/rim rhythm: rotate around broken outer
  arcs or enter the centre through a different wide opening.

Retain the host missions' authored difficulty and role vocabulary as the starting
point, not an automatic claim that new geometry is balanced at that band. Keep
introductory core arcs wall-free until the established wall lesson. Directly
selectable studies need a protected wall demonstration and a visible nonmodal
reminder in Solo and Versus; `design.lesson` metadata alone is not that reminder.
Avoid making every theme the same ring, corridor or mirrored opening in disguise.

## Evidence limits and deferred work

licensing, new final artwork, enjoyment or public availability. Links are
references, not runtime downloads.

shirt adaptations remain later candidates. In particular, Örnek needs appropriate
