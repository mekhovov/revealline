## Menus, levels and repeated listening

Use two calmer compositions per family for menus, eight full gameplay songs and two intense finales. Let motif families and instrument palettes connect menu and level identity while giving menu arrangements room and less percussion. This is a chosen design supported by the range of dedicated cues and arrangements in the official soundtrack catalogues, not a rule inferred from popularity. Explicitly chosen playlists should keep playing across menus and levels; automatic mode can choose by role/theme at a musical boundary.

90s synth is not one sound: the briefs distinguish FM leads/keys, tracker-inspired sampling, chip/pulse voices, analog pads, breakbeats and dance grooves. Metal requires convincing rhythmic articulation and developed riffs, with quieter contrast to limit fatigue. Ukrainian originals span village ensemble interaction, Carpathian dance, kobzar-inspired storytelling, restrained menu reflection and modern city/electronic fusion.

## Authenticity and rights boundaries

Keep traditional and modern Ukrainian claims distinct. Identify real instruments and any approximations. Validate ornament, regional claims and any original Ukrainian text with a capable practitioner. Instrumental first drafts avoid invented phonetic lyrics; vocal production remains possible with documented performers and language review. Avoid generic “pan-Slavic” substitutions or reducing Ukrainian identity to war, one mode, balalaika or one mountain region.

The Polyphony and broadcaster archives are listening/research sources, not presumed royalty-free sample libraries. Free instrument-production options and their specific limitations are documented in PRODUCTION.md. The 24 community tracks retained elsewhere are separate licensed compositions, never counted toward these 36 originals.
