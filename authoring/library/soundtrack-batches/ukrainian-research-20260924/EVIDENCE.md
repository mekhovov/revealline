### Cultural and musical limits

The [Ukrainian Institute's Notes from Ukraine programme](https://ui.org.ua/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/programme_notes-from-ukraine.pdf)
documents Mykola Leontovych's Shchedryk, its Ukrainian New Year song basis and the later English
Carol of the Bells adaptation. The candidate's cautious working description is **Ukrainian-melody
metal adaptation**. It is not evidence of Ukrainian traditional instrumentation or a regional
Ukrainian performance. The title and creator's rendition description identify the repertoire;
full listening must still confirm the actual melody, arrangement and absence of vocals or other
third-party material.

The composition and this recording's arrangement/performance rights must be considered separately.
Credit Leontovych's underlying Shchedryk where appropriate after musical review, preserve the
creator's release title, and do not reproduce English lyrics. Multiple Shchedryk arrangements do not
count as distinct underlying compositions or satisfy the target for a varied Ukrainian collection.

Before public admission: acquire the authorized MP3 when storage permits; preserve exact bytes and
compute SHA-256; bind the licence evidence to that recording; verify the composition/arrangement;
fully decode, measure and audition; complete Ukrainian musical review; then test in-game playback,
transitions, installation and offline playback. All remain pending.

## Hold: Jason Shaw — Shchedryk (Carol of the Bells), instrumental

The [current Commons file page](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shchedryk_(Carol_of_the_Bells)_-_Instrumental.ogg>)
and its [earliest revision, 24 December 2016](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=227375440&title=File:Shchedryk_(Carol_of_the_Bells)_-_Instrumental.ogg>)
identify Jason Shaw and claim CC BY 3.0 for the recording, separately from the underlying composition.
Both link to the creator's [signed public release](https://audionautix.com/Saved/CCrelease.jpg), dated
12 December 2010. Visual inspection of that creator-hosted document found a broad commercial-use
grant for the Audionautix collection with Jason Shaw credit.

Commons identifies the [cover-free Ogg](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bd/Shchedryk_%28Carol_of_the_Bells%29_-_Instrumental.ogg)
as uploaded `2016-12-24T15:48:54Z`, 4,374,282 bytes, 94.35467120181406 seconds, with source-reported
SHA-1 `24c7fb4f4cd7e1fd030523d9e1d4498c62e6d033`. These values were read from Commons metadata, not
verified against acquired bytes. The page's 2011 recording date is not its upload date.

However, the creator's archived [4 January 2011 homepage](https://web.archive.org/web/20110104041404id_/http://audionautix.com/)
already said the music must form part of another created work alongside its CC 3.0 claim. The
[1 January 2016 homepage](https://web.archive.org/web/20160101010944id_/http://audionautix.com:80/)
has the same limitation alongside an explicit CC BY 3.0 link. The current
[Audionautix homepage](https://audionautix.com/) retains this limitation beside CC BY 4.0.

This is a historical conflict, not simply a restriction added after the recording was licensed.
[CC BY 3.0 section 7(b)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/legalcode) preserves a valid
earlier grant when a licensor changes terms, but does not establish which conflicting grant applied
to this recording. **Hold standalone redistribution, archive publication and admin MP3 download**
until that ambiguity is resolved. Do not silently replace the source terms with an unrestricted
runtime permission. This research offers no legal guarantee.
