# Readable enemies and inexpensive animation iteration

Reviewed 2026-09-14. These are first-person production accounts and an artist's own tutorial. Their lessons inform the following decisions; they do not establish XPOSED's exact timings or sprite sizes.

Motion Twin artist Gwenaël Massé explains a visual hierarchy in which threats receive stronger contrast and brightness than the background. Consistent visual language helps recurring interactive elements remain recognizable across environments. For RevealLine, the resulting recommendation is to test enemy outlines over both opaque concealment and revealed pictures, keep the active line and warning targets readable, and use broad highlights on compact bodies. [Dead Cells color and visual hierarchy](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-giving-back-colors-to-cryptic-worlds-in-i-dead-cells-i-).

Motion Twin artist Thomas Vasseur describes approving convincing, properly timed key poses before adding transitional frames, with effects supplying impact. His production pipeline made repeated animation revisions practical. For RevealLine, retain independently replaceable bodies and components, inspect warning/attack/recovery poses before polishing, and make cosmetic timing adjustments without rewriting collision or encounter rules. This recommendation does not require adopting his 3D toolchain. [Dead Cells animation production](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-).

Pixel artist Raymond Schlitter recommends establishing sprite and screen scale early and distinguishes the frame from its occupied character pixels. Small sprites need stronger abstraction. For RevealLine, compare occupied size at actual 16/24/32-pixel frames rather than judging a large generated sheet, then produce separate compact treatment where detail collapses. [Top-down character sprites](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2019/10/21/pixelblog-22-top-down-character-sprites).

The local source preview supports that last decision: the atlas reads well at 64 pixels, but 16-pixel hunter/rover forms are difficult to identify and 24-pixel bodies need stronger contrast. Clean alpha, distinct shape and useful warning animation remain separate acceptance checks. No article, generated image or automated route proves player enjoyment.
