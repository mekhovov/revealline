## Cultural evidence and creative choices

The ministry's museum entry situates Hutsul craft in the Ukrainian Carpathian highlands and lists textile-related objects. Our blanket pattern, porch, roof and mountain location are invented, not reproductions of those objects. [Museum Fund of Ukraine](https://museum.mincult.gov.ua/museums/natsionalnyy-muzey-narodnogo-mystetstva-gutsulshchyny-ta-pokuttya-imeni-y-kobrynskogo).

A museum curator's article documents intricate Hutsul woodcarving and decorative inlay. This supports the broad craft emphasis; it does not authenticate the generated chest or post motifs, and no artist's work was supplied as a reference image. [Ukrainian Museum and Library of Stamford](<https://ukrainianmuseumlibrary.org/museum-columns/2010-PDF-columns/Vasyl-Devdiuk-Hutsul-Wood-Carver-(8-2010).pdf>).

UNESCO describes Petrykivka's fantastic flowers and natural motifs on homes and household belongings. The generated mural, bowl and birds are original digital interpretations; they do not demonstrate traditional brush technique or represent a named artist's composition. [UNESCO heritage listing](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/petrykivka-decorative-painting-as-a-phenomenon-of-the-ukrainian-ornamental-folk-art-00893).

Khortytsia Reserve describes its modern Sich complex as a generalized settlement image, including palisade defenses, towers, workshops, a church and gathering space. Our layout is a fictional riverside scene, not a replica of that complex, an archaeological plan or an exact historical event. [Khortytsia National Reserve](https://ostriv.org/tourist/zaporizka-sich/).

These primary descriptions were read on 13 September 2026. The separate hutsul.museum site returned HTTP 429 and was not used as evidence. No source photographs, paintings or game images were passed to image generation. Regional decorative accuracy remains a human review gate; the scene labels describe inspiration, not certification.
